Variety notes

About tsavorite

Green grossular garnet, colored by vanadium and chromium. Campbell Bridges found it in Tanzania in 1967 and in Kenya in 1970; it was named tsavorite in 1973 and reached jewelry buyers in 1974. Rarer than red garnet, because it needs unusual rock chemistry, and most stones are small. Singly refractive, often red through a Chelsea filter, hardness 7 to 7.5. As a species it generally isn't treated; that's about the variety, not any one stone.

Variety notes

About tourmaline

Not one mineral but a whole family of borosilicates; elbaite gives us most gem tourmaline, and liddicoatite is named for Richard T. Liddicoat. One of the widest color ranges of any gem: one pegmatite pocket can turn up several colors, and one crystal can grow pink at the core and green at the rim. Manganese is what makes it pink. Doubly refractive and strongly pleochroic, so the color shifts as you turn it. Hardness 7 to 7.5; mined worldwide, from Brazil to Namibia and Russia (GIA).
